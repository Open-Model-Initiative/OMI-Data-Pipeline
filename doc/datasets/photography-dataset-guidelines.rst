Photography Dataset Guidelines
===============================

Quality Standards
-----------------

- **Focus**: The main subject should be in focus. Various depths of field are acceptable, but the primary subject must be sharp.
- **Photography basics**: If you're new to photography, consider reading a quick guide to taking good photographs first.
- **Variety is key**: The more diverse our dataset, the better. Focus on these areas:

Subject Variety
---------------

- Include as many different subjects as possible
- If you prefer certain subjects (e.g., architecture), still aim for variety within that category
- Capture unique subjects when you have responsible access to them

Perspective Variety
-------------------

- Photograph the same object from multiple angles and distances
- Aim for as many perspectives as makes sense
- Limit to 20 images maximum of the same object (we may not be able to include more)

Lighting Variety
----------------

- Capture images in different lighting conditions (you may need to disable flash)
- Include both bright and darker conditions
- This helps train models to handle various lighting scenarios

Color and Texture Variety
--------------------------

- Include diverse colors, textures, and surfaces
- Capture everything from bright and shiny to dull and bumpy surfaces
- This is particularly important for training our VAE (Variational Autoencoder)

Priority Content Requests
--------------------------

These specific types of content would be especially valuable:

- **Global perspectives**: Cultural aesthetics, architecture, landscapes, and plant/animal life from your region
- **Historic architecture**: Older buildings are especially valuable for expanding the model's range
- **Museum exhibits**: Historic objects, artifacts, sculptures (check photography policies first)
- **People and body parts**: Hands in various poses/angles, facial details, etc. (following Hugging Face Content Policy guidelines)
